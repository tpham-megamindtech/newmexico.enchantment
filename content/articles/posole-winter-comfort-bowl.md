When the high desert turns cold and the piñon fires start, New Mexican kitchens fill with the smell of posole. This slow-simmered stew of hominy, pork, and red chile is winter comfort in a bowl — and, at the holidays, a tradition as fixed as the tree.

## What's in the pot

The base is hominy — field corn treated with lime until the kernels puff and split. Simmered for hours with pork and dried red chile, it turns into something greater than its parts: hearty, faintly smoky, and deeply satisfying.

> A pot of posole on the stove is how a New Mexican house says the holidays have arrived.

## A holiday fixture

Posole appears year-round but reaches its ceremonial peak around Christmas and New Year, when big pots feed extended families and every cook swears by their own tweak — a little oregano here, a squeeze of lime there.

## Build your own bowl

Part of the joy is the garnish: shredded cabbage, radish, onion, lime, and a warm tortilla on the side. Everyone doctors their bowl to taste, and no two tables do it quite the same.

**Field notes**

- Garnishes aren't optional — the fresh crunch balances the richness.
- It tastes even better the second day.
- Red chile is traditional, but green posole has its fans too.

Simple ingredients, hours of patience, and a table full of people to share it with — posole is less a recipe than a ritual, and it never fails to warm the coldest desert night.
