Mornings in New Mexico begin with a foil-wrapped miracle. The breakfast burrito — eggs, crispy potatoes, cheese, and chile folded into a warm flour tortilla — is the state's great equalizer, eaten by ranchers and gallery owners alike, standing up, in a hurry, and with total devotion.

## Handheld or smothered

Order it 'to go' and it comes wrapped in foil, built for the truck cab or the trailhead. Order it on a plate and it arrives 'smothered' — drowned in red or green and blanketed with cheese, a knife-and-fork affair that demands you sit down.

> A great breakfast burrito is the reason New Mexicans are cheerful before coffee.

## The fillings that matter

Purists keep it simple: egg, potato, cheese, chile. Add bacon, chorizo, or carne adovada if you must, but the chile does the heavy lifting. The potatoes should be crisp, the tortilla warm and pliable.

## Where to find the best

Every neighborhood has a champion, usually a small family spot that's been doing it the same way for decades. Follow the line of contractors and construction trucks at 7 a.m. — they know.

**Field notes**

- Smothered needs a fork; to-go is a one-handed commute food.
- Carne adovada — red-chile-marinated pork — is a worthy upgrade.
- Follow the work-truck crowd to the real institutions.

It costs a few dollars and fuels a whole morning. No wonder New Mexicans will drive across town, and defend their favorite spot to the death, for the perfect one.
