The most breathtaking thing at Carlsbad Caverns happens above ground, at dusk. From late spring through October, hundreds of thousands of Brazilian free-tailed bats pour out of the cavern's natural entrance in a swirling, smoke-like column, off to hunt insects across the desert night. It's one of North America's great wildlife spectacles.

## A river of wings

As the light fades, the bats emerge in a continuous spiral that can last well over an hour, rising out of the dark mouth of the cave and streaming into the sky. The sheer number is difficult to comprehend until you've seen it.

> Hundreds of thousands of bats pour into the dusk, and the whole crowd falls silent.

## The ranger's amphitheater

Visitors gather at the outdoor amphitheater at the cave entrance, where a ranger shares the story of the colony before the flight begins. Phones and cameras must be off — the bats navigate by more than sight, and the darkness is part of the show.

## A seasonal wonder

The bats migrate, so the flight only happens in the warmer months; the park holds special dawn programs, too, when the colony returns. Check the season and timing before making the trip.

**Field notes**

- The bat flight runs roughly late spring through October.
- No photography or phones — the darkness matters.
- Arrive early for the ranger talk and a good seat.

You come to Carlsbad for the cave, but the bat flight may be what stays with you. As the last of the colony vanishes into the darkening sky, the desert night feels suddenly, thrillingly alive.
