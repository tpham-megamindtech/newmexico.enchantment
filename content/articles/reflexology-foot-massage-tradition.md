After a day hiking the mesas or walking the galleries of Canyon Road, the part of you that suffers most is the part most often ignored: your feet. New Mexico's reflexologists are building a devoted following by giving that neglected foundation the careful, deliberate attention it deserves.

## An old practice, patiently done

Reflexology applies pressure to specific points on the feet, following a tradition that spans many cultures and centuries. Whatever you make of its claimed maps and meridians, the sensation of skilled, sustained pressure on tired feet is undeniably wonderful.

> Treat your feet well and the whole day of walking suddenly feels worthwhile.

## Why it suits a walking town

Santa Fe and Taos are made for wandering, and all that walking takes a toll. A focused foot session sends you back out onto the cobbles restored, with a spring that a general massage rarely delivers.

## What a session feels like

You stay clothed, reclined, and warm while the practitioner works methodically across each foot. Many people find it as mentally quieting as it is physically relieving, drifting close to sleep.

**Field notes**

- Perfect after a long day of hiking or gallery-hopping.
- You stay fully clothed — an easy first treatment to try.
- Book it for the end of the day, not the start.

It's an unglamorous corner of the wellness world, but ask anyone who's limped in and floated out: for a town best explored on foot, reflexology might be the smartest treatment on the menu.
