New Mexico's clay heritage runs deep, and a new generation of studio potters is building on it in fresh directions. Working from converted adobes and light-filled studios, these makers blend ancient influence with contemporary form, and the result is some of the most talked-about ceramics in the country.

## Old ground, new hands

Many of these artists dig or source local clay, tying their work to the same earth that fed centuries of Pueblo potters. The forms may be modern, but the material connection to place is deliberate and deeply felt.

> The wheel is ancient; what these potters are throwing on it is thrillingly now.

## Function meets art

Much of the movement is rooted in the functional — mugs, bowls, plates meant for daily use — elevated by glazes that echo desert light and mineral color. Restaurants across the state now plate their food on locally thrown ware.

## Where to find it

Open studios, craft fairs, and a handful of dedicated shops in Santa Fe and Taos are the best places to meet the makers and their work. Many welcome visitors to watch the wheel in action.

**Field notes**

- Look for locally dug clay and desert-inspired glazes.
- Many studios welcome visitors — call ahead.
- Functional ware makes a durable, usable souvenir.

It's a renaissance grounded in the oldest of materials, proof that in New Mexico even the newest art keeps one hand in the earth.
