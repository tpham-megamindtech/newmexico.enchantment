Seeing New Mexico's famous dark skies from a parking lot is one thing; falling asleep beneath them is another entirely. Camping in the high-desert backcountry, far from any glow of town, delivers a night sky so vivid and complete that it can genuinely change how you feel about your place in the universe.

## Skip the tent, or leave the fly off

On a clear, dry New Mexico night, many campers cowboy-camp under the open sky or leave the rainfly off, drifting to sleep as the Milky Way arcs overhead. Meteors streak past, satellites glide, and the show never stops.

> You don't watch the Milky Way from your sleeping bag so much as fall asleep inside it.

## Choosing your spot

The key is distance from light. Public lands across the state offer dispersed camping far from any town, and a little research turns up sites where the darkness is close to absolute. New moon gives the deepest black.

## Come prepared

High-desert nights are cold even in summer, and the backcountry offers no services. Pack warm layers, plenty of water, and everything you need to be self-sufficient — then settle in and look up.

**Field notes**

- Plan around the new moon for the darkest skies.
- Nights are cold at altitude even in summer — pack layers.
- Use public lands that allow dispersed camping; leave no trace.

A night under the New Mexico stars is the kind of experience people remember for the rest of their lives. Pack warm, drive far from the lights, lie back, and let the oldest light in the universe put you to sleep.
