If posole is the holiday stew, green chile stew is the everyday one. A pot of pork, potatoes, and roasted green chile simmered until soft and steaming, it's the bowl New Mexicans make when it's cold, when they're sick, or when they simply want to taste home.

## Few ingredients, big flavor

The formula is elemental: browned pork, diced potato, onion, garlic, and a lot of roasted, chopped green chile, loosened with broth. The chile does nearly all the work, which is why the stew is only as good as the harvest behind it.

> Green chile stew is less a recipe than a birthright, argued over in every kitchen in the state.

## Everyone's is different

No two families agree on the details. Some add tomato, some swear against it; some keep it brothy, others cook it nearly dry. Arguing about the right way is half the pleasure of eating it.

## The ultimate comfort

Ladled over a tortilla or eaten straight with one on the side, green chile stew is restorative in a way few dishes are. It's the first thing many New Mexicans crave when they've been away too long.

**Field notes**

- Use freshly roasted green chile whenever you can.
- It deepens overnight — make it a day ahead.
- A warm tortilla is the only utensil you really need.

It costs little, takes an afternoon, and tastes like belonging. Ask any New Mexican far from home what they miss, and a bowl of green chile stew will be near the top of the list.
