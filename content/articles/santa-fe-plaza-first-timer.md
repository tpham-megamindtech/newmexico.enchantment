Santa Fe wears its age lightly. The Plaza has been the city's living room since 1610, and a single unhurried weekend is enough to fall for its low adobe skyline, its long afternoon light, and the smell of piñon smoke that arrives the moment the sun drops.

## Start under the portal

Begin beneath the Palace of the Governors, where Native artisans lay out turquoise and silver on blankets each morning. Buy nothing on the first pass — just walk the length of the portal, learn a few names, and come back for the piece you can't stop thinking about.

> In Santa Fe you don't sightsee so much as slow down until the city comes to you.

## Walk the adobe blocks

From the Plaza, every direction rewards a wanderer. Canyon Road climbs gently past a hundred galleries; the Railyard pulls in a younger, artier crowd; and the side streets hide courtyards where hollyhocks lean against sun-warmed clay walls.

## Eat where the locals queue

You will be asked one question more than any other in this town: red or green? Order your chile 'Christmas' to get both, and don't leave without a sopaipilla drizzled with honey to put out the fire.

**Field notes**

- Base yourself within walking distance of the Plaza — parking is the only real hassle downtown.
- Afternoon light is gold from about 4 p.m.; save Canyon Road for then.
- Elevation is 7,200 feet — drink twice the water you think you need.

Two days barely scratch the surface, but they will teach you the city's rhythm: slow mornings, long lunches, and evenings that end with a glass of something under a sky gone violet.
