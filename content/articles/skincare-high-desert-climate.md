The high desert is hard on skin, and visitors feel it fast: tight, flaky, and unexpectedly sunburned within a day. New Mexico's estheticians spend their careers countering this exact combination of low humidity, fierce UV, and thin mountain air — and their advice applies to residents and travelers alike.

## The three-part assault

At altitude the sun is stronger, the air holds almost no moisture, and the elevation itself dehydrates. Skin loses water it can't easily replace, and unprotected faces burn quickly even on cool days.

> The desert will find every gap in your skincare routine — and widen it.

## The professional's playbook

Local estheticians preach hydration in layers — a humectant serum sealed with a richer moisturizer — plus daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, no exceptions. Hydrating facials that flood the skin with moisture are among the most requested treatments in the state.

## Habits that help

Drink far more water than usual, run a humidifier at night, and resist the urge to over-exfoliate dry skin. Small adjustments make an outsized difference in a climate this demanding.

**Field notes**

- Sunscreen daily, even in winter; UV is stronger at altitude.
- Layer a humectant under a heavier moisturizer.
- A bedroom humidifier is a desert dweller's secret weapon.

Adapt your routine to the altitude and your skin will forgive the desert its harshness. Ignore it, and the high desert will remind you, flake by flake, exactly where you are.
