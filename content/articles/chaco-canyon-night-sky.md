Deep in the remote San Juan Basin, the monumental stone great houses of Chaco Canyon have stood for a thousand years, built by a sophisticated Ancestral Puebloan society that watched the sky with astonishing precision. Today it's a certified dark-sky park, and its nights connect visitors directly to those ancient astronomers.

## Architecture aligned to the sky

Chaco's builders oriented walls and windows to the movements of the sun and moon, tracking solstices and lunar cycles across generations. The precision of these alignments still astonishes archaeologists and astronomers alike.

> At Chaco, you stand among ancient walls and look up at the very same sky their builders read.

## The same stars, unchanged

Because the site is so remote and so dark, the sky above Chaco looks much as it did a millennium ago. Standing among the ruins at night, you see essentially what its original inhabitants saw.

## Getting there is the challenge

Chaco's isolation — reached by long, rough dirt roads — has helped preserve both its ruins and its darkness. Come prepared, respect the fragile site, and plan to stay after dark for the night-sky programs.

**Field notes**

- Access roads are rough dirt — check conditions and prepare.
- It's a certified International Dark Sky Park.
- Respect the fragile ruins; stay on marked paths.

The drive is long and the site demands respect, but few places fuse human history and the cosmos so powerfully. At Chaco, the past and the stars feel, for a night, like the same thing.
