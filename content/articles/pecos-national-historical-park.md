An easy hour from Santa Fe, the grasslands of Pecos hold the outlines of a vanished city. Once one of the largest pueblos in the Southwest and a great trading crossroads, Pecos today is a quiet park where ruined walls and mission arches tell a thousand-year story.

## A trading crossroads

At its height, Pecos Pueblo housed some 2,000 people and sat astride the trade route between the Pueblo world and the Plains tribes. Cornmeal and cotton moved one way; buffalo hides and flint the other.

> Stand in the mission nave and the wind does the narrating.

## The mission walls

The roofless adobe walls of a Spanish colonial mission church still rise from the ridge, their scale startling against the open sky. An easy 1.25-mile loop trail connects the pueblo mounds and the mission with interpretive signs throughout.

## Rivers and battlefields

The park also protects a stretch of the Pecos River and, remarkably, the Glorieta Pass Civil War battlefield — layers of American history folded into one grassy valley.

**Field notes**

- Start at the visitor center for context before the loop.
- The trail is gentle and mostly flat — good for families.
- Combine with lunch in the village of Pecos nearby.

There are no crowds here, no gift-shop bustle — just grass, wind, and the eloquent silence of walls that outlived the city they once held.
