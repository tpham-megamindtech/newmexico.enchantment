In New Mexico, chile is not a topping — it is the point. The state legislature made 'Red or green?' the official state question, and any server will ask it the moment you order. Learning to answer is the first step to eating well here.

## Same pod, two destinies

Red and green come from the same plant; the difference is ripeness and preparation. Green chile is picked young, roasted, and peeled, giving a bright, vegetal heat. Red is left to ripen and dry, then simmered into a deep, earthy sauce.

> You don't choose New Mexican food; you choose red or green, and it chooses you.

## How to answer

There's no wrong choice, but there are strategies. Green tends toward fresh and fiery; red toward rich and smoky. Can't decide? Say 'Christmas,' and your plate arrives half and half — a local trick that marks you as someone who's paying attention.

## Heat and terroir

Chile is agricultural, so heat varies by harvest and by field. Hatch, in the state's south, is the most famous name, but northern chile from Chimayó has its own devoted following. Ask your server which is hotter that week.

**Field notes**

- Milk, not water, tames the burn — order a glass if you're unsure.
- 'Christmas' gets you both sauces on one plate.
- Roasting season perfumes the whole state each autumn.

Master the question and you hold the key to the whole cuisine. Everything else — the enchiladas, the burritos, the stews — is just a delivery system for the answer.
