New Mexico sits at a delicious crossroads, and its tacos show it. Neither strictly Mexican nor Tex-Mex, they carry the state's own accent — red-chile-marinated pork, local cheeses, and a griddle culture shaped by centuries at the frontier's edge.

## Carne adovada, the local hero

The filling to seek out is carne adovada: pork slow-cooked in red chile until it falls apart into a rich, tangy tangle. Folded into a warm tortilla with onion and cilantro, it's a taco that could only come from here.

> Order carne adovada once and every other taco filling starts to feel like a compromise.

## North versus south

Closer to the border, the influence runs more Sonoran — flour tortillas, grilled meats, a hot-dog-and-taco street culture. Up north, corn and red chile dominate. The state contains multitudes.

## The griddle rules

Wherever you are, look for a busy flat-top and hand-warmed tortillas. A great New Mexican taco is a simple thing done attentively, not a tower of toppings.

**Field notes**

- Carne adovada is the signature — start there.
- Southern New Mexico leans Sonoran; the north leans red-chile.
- Warm tortillas are the tell of a serious kitchen.

Come with your assumptions about tacos and leave with new ones. New Mexico doesn't copy its neighbors — it cooks in an accent entirely its own.
