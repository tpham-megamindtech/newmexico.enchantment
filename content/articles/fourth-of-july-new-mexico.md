The Fourth of July looks a little different under a high-desert sky. Across New Mexico, small-town parades wind past adobe plazas, fireworks burst against mountain silhouettes, and the smell of green chile on the grill mingles with the gunpowder. It's Americana with an unmistakably local accent.

## Plaza parades

Many towns still throw the classic small-town parade — fire trucks, horses, kids on decorated bikes — around a historic plaza. It's wholesome, unpretentious, and a wonderful window into local life.

> Only in New Mexico does the Fourth of July smell like gunpowder and roasting green chile.

## Fireworks with a backdrop

The state's dramatic terrain gives its fireworks displays a spectacular stage, whether framed by the Sandias, the Sangre de Cristos, or an open desert horizon. Wildfire risk sometimes shifts plans, so check locally.

## Chile on the grill

No New Mexico gathering is complete without chile, and the Fourth is no exception. Green chile cheeseburgers, roasted corn, and cold drinks turn the holiday into the year's great backyard feast.

**Field notes**

- Check for fire restrictions, which can alter displays in dry years.
- Small-town plaza parades are the most charming celebrations.
- Bring layers — desert evenings cool off fast after sunset.

Between the parade and the fireworks, with a chile-smothered burger in hand and the mountains going purple at dusk, the Fourth in New Mexico feels both entirely American and entirely its own.
