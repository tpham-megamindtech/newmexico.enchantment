Rise above the low adobe rooftops of downtown Santa Fe and one building breaks the spell: the honey-colored stone of the Cathedral Basilica of St. Francis of Assisi, French-Romanesque and utterly unlike everything around it. That contrast is exactly why it fascinates.

## A bishop's European dream

Archbishop Jean-Baptiste Lamy built it in the 1800s in the style of his French homeland, importing stone and ambition to a frontier town of mud brick. Willa Cather turned him into a novel; the building remains his monument.

> In a city that insists on adobe, the cathedral is a beautiful act of defiance.

## Inside the stone

The interior is cool and hushed, with stained glass from France and, tucked in a side chapel, a wooden statue of the Virgin that is among the oldest representations of the Madonna in the United States.

## The steps and the city

Outside, the cathedral steps are a favorite gathering spot, and the short walk back to the Plaza passes some of downtown's best galleries and cafés. Photographers love the façade in early light.

**Field notes**

- Services are ongoing; visit quietly and respectfully.
- Morning light on the east-facing stone is best for photos.
- It's a two-minute walk from the Plaza — combine easily.

You may come to Santa Fe for the adobe, but the cathedral is a reminder that this city has always been a crossroads of very different dreams.
