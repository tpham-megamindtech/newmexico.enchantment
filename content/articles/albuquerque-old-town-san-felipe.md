Everything in Old Town Albuquerque orbits one building. San Felipe de Neri has stood on the plaza since the 1790s, outlasting Spanish rule, Mexican rule, the arrival of the railroad, and the sprawl of a modern city. To understand Albuquerque, start at its oldest door.

## Two centuries of continuity

The parish was founded in 1706 with the city itself; the current adobe church replaced an earlier one that collapsed. Mass has been said here almost without interruption ever since — a rare thread of continuity in a fast-changing city.

> The city changed around it; the church simply stayed, and stayed, and stayed.

## Adobe that endures

The walls are several feet thick, the twin towers a later Gothic-Revival addition. Inside, pressed-tin ceilings and a carved altar reward a quiet look, and a small museum tells the parish's long story.

## A living plaza

Step outside and the plaza still functions as it always has: a shaded gathering place, busy with festivals, weddings, and the slow foot traffic of a neighborhood that refuses to be only a tourist attraction.

**Field notes**

- The small parish museum is free and often overlooked.
- Respect services — this is an active church, not a museum piece.
- Old Town's best galleries are steps from the doors.

Spend ten minutes in its cool nave and Albuquerque's frenetic modern face falls away, replaced by the patient, mud-brick heart that started it all.
