Some hikes deliver more wonder per mile than others, and few beat Kasha-Katuwe Tent Rocks. Named for the tent-shaped hoodoos that give it its Keresan name, this compact landscape squeezes a slot canyon, towering cones, and a sweeping mesa view into a few unforgettable miles.

## Through the slot

The signature trail threads a narrow slot canyon where the walls close to arm's width and soar overhead. Sunlight filters down in shafts, and the smoothed stone curves have the feel of a natural cathedral.

> Squeeze through the slot canyon and emerge into a forest of stone tents — it never gets old.

## The tent rocks themselves

The cones formed as soft volcanic ash eroded away beneath harder cap-stones, leaving pale, tapering towers. Some still wear their protective boulder like a hat, a snapshot of erosion caught mid-act.

## The climb to the top

The trail rises steeply to a mesa rim with a commanding view over the hoodoo field and out to distant mountains. It's a workout at altitude, but the payoff is one of the best vistas in the state.

**Field notes**

- Check access before you go; the site has had closures.
- Bring water and sun protection — shade is scarce up top.
- The slot canyon is narrow; go light on the daypack.

Short, strange, and spectacular, Tent Rocks rewards a few hours of effort with scenery you'll struggle to describe afterward. It's a reminder that New Mexico hides wonders down its quietest dirt roads.
