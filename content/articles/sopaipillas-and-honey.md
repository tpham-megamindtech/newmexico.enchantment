Every New Mexican meal has a happy ending, and it is shaped like a pillow. Sopaipillas — squares of dough fried until they puff hollow and golden — arrive at the table hot, ready to be torn open and filled with honey. They are also, conveniently, the best cure for too much chile.

## How the magic happens

A simple dough hits hot oil and, within seconds, balloons into a crisp, air-filled cushion. The inside stays tender; the outside shatters gently. Timing is everything, and good cooks make it look effortless.

> No meal in New Mexico is truly over until the honey bear comes out.

## Sweet and savory

Most often they come as dessert, torn at one corner and flooded with honey. But sopaipillas also appear savory, stuffed with beans and chile as a meal in themselves — proof of how versatile a puff of fried dough can be.

## The chile antidote

There's a practical genius here, too. When the green chile has set your mouth ablaze, a honey-filled sopaipilla is the traditional and wonderfully effective way to put out the fire.

**Field notes**

- Eat them hot — they deflate and toughen as they cool.
- The squeeze-bottle honey bear is part of the ritual.
- Savory stuffed versions make a full, cheap lunch.

Simple, humble, and endlessly comforting, the sopaipilla is New Mexico in miniature: unpretentious on the outside, warm and generous within.
