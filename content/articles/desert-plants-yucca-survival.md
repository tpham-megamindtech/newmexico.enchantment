To thrive in New Mexico, a plant has to be tough. The state's flora — from the spiky yucca that is its official flower to the prickly pear and the fragrant sage — are masters of desert survival, and learning their names turns any high-desert walk into a richer, more rewarding experience.

## The yucca, state flower and survivor

The yucca sends up dramatic spikes of creamy blossoms and endures the harshest conditions with deep roots and tough, sword-like leaves. Long a source of food, fiber, and soap for the region's peoples, it's a fitting emblem for the state.

> Every plant in the high desert is a small, stubborn masterpiece of survival.

## Water-savers everywhere

Cacti like the prickly pear store water in fleshy pads; sagebrush and chamisa cut water loss with silvery, resinous leaves. Every species here has its own ingenious strategy for surviving on almost nothing.

## The desert in bloom

After the rare rains, the desert can erupt in wildflowers, a fleeting spectacle that rewards good timing. Even in dry spells, the subtle greens and grays of the high desert have a quiet beauty all their own.

**Field notes**

- The yucca is New Mexico's official state flower.
- Don't touch — many desert plants defend themselves fiercely.
- Time a spring visit right and the desert may bloom.

Learn to name them and the desert stops looking barren and starts looking ingenious. New Mexico's plants are proof that beauty and toughness aren't opposites — out here, they're the same thing.
