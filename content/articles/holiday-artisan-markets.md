The holiday season transforms New Mexico's plazas into glowing marketplaces. As the farolitos — those little paper-bag lanterns — line the adobe walls, artisan markets fill with handmade gifts, and the cold evenings take on a warmth that has nothing to do with temperature.

## Handmade, not mass-made

The holiday markets are a chance to buy directly from the makers — pottery, jewelry, textiles, tinwork, and treats — supporting local artists and taking home gifts with genuine stories attached, rather than something off a big-box shelf.

> Under a canopy of farolitos, holiday shopping stops feeling like a chore.

## The glow of farolitos

Strings of farolitos (or luminarias, depending on whom you ask) outline rooftops and pathways, their candlelight flickering against the snow. The effect turns even a simple shopping stroll into something quietly enchanting.

## Warm traditions

Vendors ladle out hot cider and biscochitos, carolers appear, and the whole scene leans into a distinctly New Mexican Christmas. Bundle up, take your time, and let the season work on you.

**Field notes**

- Buy directly from makers for gifts with real provenance.
- Evenings glow brightest — go after dark for the farolitos.
- Dress warmly and pause for cider and biscochitos.

Between the handmade treasures and the candlelit glow, New Mexico's holiday markets make the season feel handmade too. It's shopping as celebration — unhurried, warm, and deeply rooted in place.
