In a state this steeped in clay, it feels almost rude not to try. Beginner pottery classes have multiplied across Santa Fe and Taos, and spending an afternoon with your hands in wet earth turns out to be one of the most grounding, unexpectedly moving things a visitor can do here.

## Humbling and delightful

The wheel does not care about your résumé. Centering a lump of clay is harder than it looks, and the first lopsided bowl is a small triumph. Instructors are patient, and the mess is entirely the point.

> Your first bowl will be terrible, and you will treasure it forever.

## A meditative hour

There's a reason people describe throwing pots as meditative. The focus required leaves no room for anything else, and an hour vanishes in the quiet rhythm of hands, water, and spinning clay.

## Take home your own

Most studios fire and glaze your pieces and ship them later, so you leave with a wonky, wonderful souvenir made by your own hands — worth more, somehow, than anything off a shelf.

**Field notes**

- No experience needed — beginner classes assume none.
- Wear clothes you don't mind getting muddy.
- Ask about firing and shipping for your finished pieces.

You'll leave with clay under your nails and a new respect for every potter in the state. It may be the most hands-on souvenir New Mexico has to offer.
