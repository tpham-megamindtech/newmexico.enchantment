Everyone comes to New Mexico for the sun, and they're missing something. When snow settles on the Sangre de Cristo Mountains and the aspens stand bare against a hard blue sky, the roads empty out and the north country turns quiet, crystalline, and unforgettable.

## Roads to yourself

The scenic routes around Taos that crawl with cars in summer belong to almost no one in deep winter. Drive prepared — chains, water, a full tank — and the reward is a mountain landscape you nearly have to yourself.

> The desert state keeps its best secret for the coldest months.

## Villages in snow

The adobe villages of the high country look their most timeless under a dusting of white, woodsmoke rising from every chimney and chile ristras bright against the walls. Stop for green-chile stew and let the car warm up.

## End the day in hot water

Northern New Mexico's geology gives it natural hot springs, and there is no finer end to a cold day's driving than sinking into steaming water while snow falls on your shoulders.

**Field notes**

- Carry winter gear and check road conditions before high passes.
- Days are short — plan to be off mountain roads by dark.
- Some hot springs require reservations in winter; call ahead.

Come in winter and you'll trade the crowds for silence, the heat for clarity, and the ordinary postcard for something far more rare.
