People have sought out New Mexico's mineral waters for centuries, drawn by springs bubbling up warm and rich with minerals from deep in the earth. Long before the word 'spa' meant anything here, this was the state's original wellness draw — and a network of retreats keeps the tradition flowing.

## Water with history

Indigenous peoples and later settlers alike valued these springs, and some sites have welcomed visitors for well over a century. The specific mineral blends vary from source to source, each with its own devoted following.

> In a desert, warm water rising from the ground has always felt like a small miracle.

## The modern retreat

Today's mineral retreats pair the soaking pools with treatments, quiet lodging, and a deliberate slowness. Days revolve around the water: soak, rest, eat well, soak again, sleep deeply.

## The ritual of return

Regulars speak of these places almost reverently, returning year after year. Whether the benefit is mineral or simply the deep rest, the effect is real enough to keep the pools full across generations.

**Field notes**

- Different springs offer different mineral profiles — ask which.
- Many retreats require reservations; walk-ins are limited.
- Build in unstructured time; the soaking is the itinerary.

Modern spas may have arrived, but the mineral springs came first, and they endure. Sink into one and you're joining a line of seekers that stretches back long before New Mexico was ever on a map.
