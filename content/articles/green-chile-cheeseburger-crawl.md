Ask a New Mexican for their desert-island meal and a surprising number will skip the enchiladas entirely and name the green chile cheeseburger. It's humble, it's everywhere, and at its best it is transcendent: a griddled patty, melting cheese, and a fistful of roasted green chile.

## The anatomy of a great one

It starts with fresh-roasted green chile, chopped and generous — never from a can if the kitchen respects itself. Add a well-seared patty, cheese melted to lace, and a soft bun that can hold the whole juicy mess together for at least a few bites.

> Forget the white tablecloth — the soul of New Mexican food is a chile-smothered burger.

## Dives beat destinations

The best versions rarely come from fancy rooms. Look for gas-station grills, small-town cafés, and drive-ins where the chile is local and the line is long. New Mexico even maintains an official trail connecting the contenders.

## How to order

Ask how hot the chile is running this week; a good cook will know. Get it 'all the way,' grab extra napkins, and accept that you will need both hands and a nap afterward.

**Field notes**

- Small-town cafés often out-cook the big-city names.
- Ask for the day's chile heat level before committing.
- Fries with green chile and cheese are non-negotiable.

There's no ranking that everyone will agree with, and that's the joy of it — the green chile cheeseburger crawl is an argument New Mexicans are happy to keep having forever.
