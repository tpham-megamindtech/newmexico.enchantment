When the chile harvest comes in each fall, New Mexico celebrates the only way it knows how — with a festival. From the famous gathering in Hatch to smaller village fiestas across the north, the harvest season fills plazas with roasting chile, live music, and the year's proudest produce.

## The smell of the season

These festivals are announced by scent long before sight: the smoky perfume of green chile roasting by the sackful. Vendors sell it fresh, cooked into everything, and by the bushel to take home and freeze.

> A harvest festival in chile season is the most fragrant party in America.

## More than chile

Alongside the star ingredient come the trappings of a good country fair — craft booths, music, carnival food, and contests. The chile is the reason, but the community gathering is the heart.

## Hatch and beyond

Hatch draws the biggest crowds over Labor Day weekend, but nearly every chile-growing community marks the harvest in its own way. Following the smaller festivals is a delicious tour through rural New Mexico.

**Field notes**

- The Hatch Chile Festival peaks over Labor Day weekend.
- Buy a roasted sack to take home and freeze for the year.
- Smaller village festivals offer a more local experience.

Eat until you're pleasantly on fire, buy more chile than you can carry, and soak up the community spirit. In New Mexico, the harvest festival is where food, culture, and celebration become one glorious, smoky whole.
