New Mexico can overwhelm an art lover. Santa Fe alone holds hundreds of galleries, and the instinct is to race through as many as possible. But the museum educators here quietly preach the opposite: that the richest experience comes not from seeing more art, but from seeing one work slowly and completely.

## The ten-minute experiment

Educators suggest choosing a single piece and staying with it for ten full minutes — far longer than the few seconds most visitors give. It feels uncomfortable at first, then something shifts: details surface, and the work begins to open up.

> You don't need to see all the art; you need to truly see some of it.

## Questions over conclusions

Slow looking is less about knowing the right answer than about asking better questions. What's happening here? Why did the artist choose this color, this scale? Curiosity, not expertise, is what deepens the encounter.

## Less, but more

Trade the checklist of galleries for a handful of real encounters and you'll remember far more. One painting truly seen outlasts a hundred glanced at on a hurried afternoon.

**Field notes**

- Pick one piece and stay with it for a full ten minutes.
- Ask questions of the work rather than reaching for answers.
- Fewer galleries, more attention — you'll remember more.

In a state this rich with art, the discipline of slowing down is the greatest gift you can give yourself. Look less, but look deeper, and New Mexico's galleries will repay you many times over.
