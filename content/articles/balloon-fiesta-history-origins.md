The International Balloon Fiesta didn't begin as a global phenomenon. It started in 1972 as a modest birthday promotion for a local radio station, with just thirteen balloons launching from a shopping-mall parking lot. Half a century later, it's the most photographed event on the planet.

## A humble launch

That first gathering was small and improvised, but the idea caught. Albuquerque's ideal flying conditions and enthusiastic community meant the event grew quickly, adding balloons and spectators year after year.

> It began with thirteen balloons and a radio promotion; it became a wonder of the world.

## Becoming the world capital

As the Fiesta grew, so did the city's identity as the ballooning capital of the world. Pilots relocated here, manufacturers set up shop, and the sport wove itself permanently into Albuquerque's culture.

## Fifty years on

Today the Fiesta spans nine days and hundreds of balloons, supported by a small army of volunteers and crew. Yet it retains a surprisingly grassroots, community feel — a big event that still remembers its scrappy origins.

**Field notes**

- The first Fiesta launched in 1972 with just 13 balloons.
- Albuquerque's flying conditions drove its rapid growth.
- Volunteers remain the backbone of the modern event.

From a parking-lot promotion to a bucket-list pilgrimage, the Fiesta's story is Albuquerque's own — proof that the biggest traditions often start with the smallest, sparkiest ideas.
