Not every New Mexican classic is refined, and thank goodness for that. Frito pie — corn chips smothered in red chile, cheese, and onion, sometimes served right in the split-open bag — is the state's great guilty pleasure, and its fans defend it with a straight face.

## The glorious mess

The canonical version is almost absurd in its simplicity: a snack bag of corn chips slit down the side, flooded with red chile con carne, then crowned with shredded cheese and diced onion and eaten with a plastic fork on the go.

> Frito pie proves New Mexican food doesn't have to be fancy to be fiercely loved.

## Fairgrounds and lunch counters

You'll find it at state fairs, ballgames, school cafeterias, and an old Santa Fe lunch counter or two that claims to have invented it. Everyone has a version, and the debates over its origins are affectionately unresolved.

## Elevated, if you insist

Some kitchens now plate a fancier take with house-made chile and better cheese, but purists argue that half the charm is eating it out of the bag. Either way, the red chile is what makes or breaks it.

**Field notes**

- The in-the-bag version is the authentic street format.
- It lives or dies on the quality of the red chile.
- Peak availability: any state fair or Friday-night ballgame.

It will never win a fine-dining award, and it doesn't want to. Frito pie is New Mexico letting its hair down — cheap, messy, and completely beloved.
