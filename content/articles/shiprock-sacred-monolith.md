Drive the empty highways of northwestern New Mexico and you'll see it long before you reach it: Shiprock, a jagged volcanic monolith rising nearly 1,500 feet from an otherwise flat desert. Sacred to the Navajo people, it dominates the horizon for miles — and it asks to be met with reverence.

## Born of fire

Shiprock is the throat of an ancient volcano, its softer surroundings long since eroded away to leave the hardened core standing alone. Radiating dikes of black rock run outward like spokes, adding to its otherworldly presence.

> Shiprock doesn't sit in the landscape so much as preside over it.

## Sacred ground

Known as Tsé Bitʼaʼí, 'the rock with wings,' it holds deep significance in Navajo tradition. Climbing it is prohibited out of respect, and the surrounding land is part of the Navajo Nation. Admire it from the roads and viewpoints, and leave it undisturbed.

## The light show

At sunrise and sunset the monolith glows, its silhouette shifting from black to burning gold against enormous skies. It's one of the most dramatic and humbling sights in a state full of them.

**Field notes**

- Do not climb — it's sacred and climbing is prohibited.
- View respectfully from public roads and pullouts.
- Sunrise and sunset offer the most dramatic light.

You can't summit it and you shouldn't try. But to stand on the desert floor and watch Shiprock catch the last light is to feel, keenly, the deep and sacred age of this land.
