Of all the treatments on a New Mexico spa menu, hot-stone massage feels the most native. Smooth basalt stones — the same volcanic rock that built these mountains — are heated and laid along the body, and at 7,000 feet, where muscles clench against the cold and dry air, the warmth lands especially deep.

## Stone from the same ground

The basalt used is dense and holds heat beautifully, warming muscle far below the surface. There's a pleasing symmetry to being worked on with the very rock that forms the surrounding peaks.

> There's a rightness to being warmed by the same stone the mountains are made of.

## Altitude and tension

The high desert is hard on the body — thin air, low humidity, cold nights. Therapists here often build treatments around that reality, using heat to coax open the tension that altitude and dryness quietly create.

## What to expect

Stones are placed and also used as extensions of the therapist's hands, gliding warm over the back and shoulders. Hydrate well before and after; at this elevation, it matters more than you'd think.

**Field notes**

- Drink extra water before and after at altitude.
- Tell your therapist about any pressure preferences up front.
- Give yourself time to rest afterward — don't rush back out.

It's a treatment offered the world over, but in New Mexico's thin, dry air it feels less like a luxury and more like exactly what the body was asking for.
