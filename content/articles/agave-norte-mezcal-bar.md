The margarita has always had a home in New Mexico, but a new generation of bartenders is pushing further. At Agave Norte — a fictional bar standing in for a very real trend — the drinks taste of the desert itself: smoky mezcal, prickly-pear syrup, piñon, and the ever-present whisper of chile.

## Beyond the margarita

The classic lime-and-tequila version is here and perfect, but the menu roams: mezcal smoked over local wood, cocktails sweetened with prickly-pear or wild plum, and a chile-salt rim that sneaks up on you three sips in.

> The best New Mexican cocktail tastes like the smell of the desert after rain.

## Ingredients from the land

The bar forages and sources locally — juniper, piñon, desert botanicals — treating the high desert as a pantry. Even the ice program is fussed over, because at this altitude drinks warm fast.

## A room with a glow

Low light, adobe walls, and a long copper bar make it feel like a speakeasy that grew out of the ground. It fills after dark with a crowd that takes its drinking seriously but never solemnly.

**Field notes**

- Try a mezcal drink before defaulting to the margarita.
- The chile-salt rim builds heat slowly — pace yourself.
- Ask what's been foraged that week.

Agave Norte is a fiction, but push open the door of the right Albuquerque bar and you'll find the real thing: the flavors of the high desert, shaken, smoked, and served over a single perfect cube.
