It sounds counterintuitive to seek out heat in a state this sunny, but New Mexico's sauna and heat-therapy studios are thriving. Behind cedar doors and glowing salt walls, a growing crowd is discovering what many cultures have always known: that deliberate, controlled heat is a genuine tonic.

## What the heat does

A good sweat raises the heart rate, loosens muscle, and leaves a pleasant, wrung-out calm in its wake. Enthusiasts talk about better sleep and clearer heads; at minimum, the deep warmth is a balm after a cold high-desert day.

> A hard sweat in winter leaves you feeling scrubbed clean from the inside out.

## The salt-wall trend

Many local studios pair the sauna with backlit Himalayan salt walls, prized as much for their warm amber glow as for any claimed benefit. The effect is undeniably soothing — a room that feels like sitting inside a lantern.

## Use it wisely

Start with shorter sessions, hydrate hard, and step out the moment you feel lightheaded. The pros alternate heat with cool-downs and never treat endurance as the goal.

**Field notes**

- Hydrate before and after — the dry air compounds fluid loss.
- Begin with short sessions and build up slowly.
- Alternate heat with cool rest for the full effect.

Step out flushed and light, pull the cold air into your lungs, and you'll understand the appeal. In New Mexico, even in a land of sun, the sauna has found a devoted home.
