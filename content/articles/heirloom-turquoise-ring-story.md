Ask a New Mexican about a favorite piece of turquoise and you'll rarely hear about a purchase — you'll hear about a person. Rings and cuffs here are passed from grandmother to grandchild, given to mark weddings and graduations, worn until the silver thins. The stone becomes a vessel for a family's story.

## Jewelry as memory

A turquoise ring often arrives with a history attached: whose it was, what it marked, how it came to you. That provenance can matter more than the stone's grade, turning modest jewelry into treasured heirloom.

> Here a turquoise ring isn't bought so much as inherited, given, and lived in.

## Marking milestones

Locally, significant pieces are given at thresholds — coming of age, marriage, achievement. Choosing the stone becomes part of the ritual, and the piece then carries that moment forever forward.

## Wear, don't store

These are objects meant to be lived in, not locked away. The soft glow of well-worn silver and a stone darkened by years of contact with skin are, to New Mexicans, marks of love, not damage.

**Field notes**

- Provenance and meaning can outweigh a stone's technical grade.
- Significant pieces are traditionally given to mark milestones.
- Well-worn silver and stone are signs of a life, not flaws.

Buy a piece of turquoise in New Mexico and you may be starting an heirloom of your own. Wear it, pass it on, and let it gather the story it was always meant to hold.
