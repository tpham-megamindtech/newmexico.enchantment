Behind a heavy wooden door off Canyon Road, Casa del Farol has quietly become Santa Fe's most talked-about table. Chef Marisol Vega — a fictional composite of the region's new guard — builds a nine-course tasting menu that treats heirloom chile and blue corn with the reverence usually reserved for truffles.

## Terroir on a plate

Vega sources within a day's drive: Chimayó chile, Pueblo-grown blue corn, lamb from the northern mesas, piñon foraged in the fall. The menu changes with what the high desert offers, and each course names its farm.

> Vega cooks the food of her grandmother's kitchen — with the precision of a jeweler.

## Familiar flavors, unfamiliar forms

A single perfect ravioli of green chile and goat cheese; a posole reimagined as a delicate broth; a blue-corn dessert dusted with piñon. The reference points are comforting, but the execution is anything but rustic.

## The room itself

Thick adobe walls, a kiva fireplace, and candlelight make the space feel like dinner in someone's very elegant home. Reservations open a month out and vanish within the hour.

**Field notes**

- Reservations release monthly and go fast; set a reminder.
- The wine pairing leans into high-desert and Southwestern bottles.
- Ask for the kiva-fireplace table in winter.

Casa del Farol is fictional, but the movement it represents is real: a generation of New Mexican chefs proving that the state's humble ingredients can hold their own at the highest table.
