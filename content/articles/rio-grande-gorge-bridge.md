West of Taos the sagebrush plain runs flat and unremarkable until, without warning, the earth splits open. The Rio Grande Gorge Bridge spans that gash 650 feet above the water, and walking to its center is a small, thrilling act of courage.

## The view straight down

Pedestrian walkways run along both sides of the bridge, with viewing platforms that jut over the void. Look up and the mesa stretches to the mountains; look down and the river is a thin green ribbon far, far below.

> The bridge doesn't cross the landscape so much as float above a wound in it.

## A gorge carved by time

The Rio Grande has been cutting this canyon for millions of years, and the raw basalt walls record every layer. Rafters run the whitewater at the bottom in season; from the bridge they look like specks.

## Golden hour on the mesa

Time your visit for late afternoon, when the low sun rakes across the gorge and sets the western wall on fire. The mesa light out here is famous among painters for a reason.

**Field notes**

- Hold onto hats and phones — the wind funnels hard through the gorge.
- A small vendors' market often sets up at the parking area on weekends.
- Pair with a visit to Taos Pueblo, fifteen minutes east.

Few roadside stops in America deliver a jolt like this one. Walk to the middle, grip the rail, and let the desert remind you how small you are.
