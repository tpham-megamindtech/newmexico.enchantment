Albuquerque gets a reputation as a highway city, but its heart is older and gentler than the freeways suggest. Old Town, founded in 1706, wraps a shaded plaza in adobe, and a slow morning here is the loveliest way to meet the Duke City.

## The plaza and San Felipe de Neri

The old church of San Felipe de Neri has anchored the plaza since the 1790s, its twin towers rising over a green square ringed with portales. Benches under the trees invite you to sit, watch, and do very little.

> Old Town moves at plaza speed — which is to say, no speed at all.

## Shops, chile, and silver

The surrounding lanes are thick with trading posts, chile shops, and galleries. Some are touristy; the best sell genuine Pueblo and Navajo work. Take your time and ask questions.

## Breakfast first

No Albuquerque morning is complete without a breakfast burrito smothered in green chile. Order it 'wet,' grab extra napkins, and understand at last why locals are so evangelical about their chile.

**Field notes**

- Free parking is easier here than downtown Santa Fe.
- The nearby ABQ BioPark makes an easy add-on with kids.
- Evenings during the holidays glow with thousands of luminarias.

By the time the plaza fills with midday sun, you'll have the measure of Albuquerque: unhurried, unpretentious, and quietly in love with its own long history.
