Western New Mexico trades the pale gypsum and black basalt of elsewhere for something warmer: bold red sandstone cliffs that rise above the high desert and glow like embers at sunset. This is red rock country, sacred to the Zuni people and among the most quietly spectacular landscapes in the state.

## Cliffs the color of fire

The deep red comes from iron in the ancient sandstone, and in the low light of morning and evening the cliffs seem to burn against the sky. It's a landscape that photographs beautifully and lingers in memory.

> At dusk the red cliffs don't just catch the light — they seem to hold fire within them.

## A living cultural landscape

This is the homeland of the Zuni, whose connection to the land runs back countless generations. Much of the area holds cultural significance, and respectful, low-impact visiting is essential.

## Off the beaten path

Far from the state's tourist corridors, red rock country sees few visitors, which is much of its appeal. The quiet, the color, and the sense of remoteness combine into something special.

**Field notes**

- Much of the land is culturally significant — visit respectfully.
- Morning and evening light set the red cliffs aglow.
- It's remote; come prepared and self-sufficient.

Off the usual routes and gloriously uncrowded, the red rock country of the west offers a different palette and a deeper quiet. Come at golden hour, watch the cliffs ignite, and understand why this land is held sacred.
