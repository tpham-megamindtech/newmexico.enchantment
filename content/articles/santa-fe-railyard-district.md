If the Plaza is Santa Fe's history, the Railyard is its present. Built around the restored 1880s rail depot, this walkable district trades adobe romance for warehouse galleries, a buzzing market, and a younger energy the old city sometimes lacks.

## Contemporary art with an edge

The Railyard's converted warehouses house some of the most ambitious galleries in the Southwest, alongside SITE Santa Fe, whose rotating exhibitions pull the international art conversation into New Mexico.

> Old rails, new ideas — the Railyard is where Santa Fe experiments.

## Saturday at the market

The Santa Fe Farmers Market fills the plaza each weekend with high-desert produce, roasted chile in season, and growers who'll happily talk your ear off about heirloom beans. Come hungry and early.

## Ride the Rail Runner

The bright commuter train still runs from here down to Albuquerque, making a car-free day trip genuinely easy. The depot itself, with its water tower landmark, anchors the whole district.

**Field notes**

- Saturday mornings are peak; go early for parking and produce.
- Check SITE Santa Fe's calendar before you visit.
- The Rail Runner to Albuquerque is scenic and cheap.

Spend a morning here and you'll see a Santa Fe that isn't preserved in amber — it's still being built, one converted warehouse at a time.
