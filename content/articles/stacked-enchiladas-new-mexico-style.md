Order enchiladas almost anywhere else and they arrive rolled. Order them in New Mexico and you may get something startling: flat corn tortillas stacked like pancakes, layered with cheese and onion, smothered in chile, and finished with a fried egg on top. This is the New Mexican way.

## Stacked, not rolled

The stacked enchilada is a regional signature. Tortillas are dipped in chile and layered flat with cheese and chopped onion between them, then baked or plated and sauced. It eats more like a savory cake than a wrap.

> In New Mexico even the enchilada refuses to do things the way the rest of the country does.

## The egg on top

The traditional crown is a fried egg, its runny yolk breaking into the chile below. It's optional but beloved, and skipping it marks you as a newcomer. Add pinto beans and posole on the side and you have the classic combination plate.

## Red, green, or Christmas

As with everything here, the chile choice defines the dish. Red brings earthy depth; green brings bright heat; Christmas gives you both, poured over opposite corners of the stack.

**Field notes**

- Add the fried egg — it's the authentic move.
- The combo plate with beans and posole is the full experience.
- Flat-stacked means every bite gets equal chile.

It looks unfamiliar the first time it lands on your table. By the last forkful, dragging tortilla through chile and yolk, you'll wonder why anyone bothers rolling them at all.
