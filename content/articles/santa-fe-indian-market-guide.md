For one weekend each August, the Santa Fe Plaza becomes the center of the Native art world. Indian Market draws the finest Indigenous artists from across the country and tens of thousands of visitors, and navigating its booths of jewelry, pottery, and textiles is a thrilling, slightly overwhelming rite of passage.

## The best, in one place

Juried and fiercely competitive, the Market gathers museum-quality work you'd otherwise chase across a dozen states. Silver and turquoise jewelry, in particular, appears here at a level rarely seen in any single place.

> For one August weekend, the Plaza holds the finest Native art in the country.

## Meet the makers

The real magic is buying directly from the artists, hearing the meaning behind a design, and supporting Indigenous makers without any middleman. Prize-winning pieces sell within minutes of the ribbons going up.

## How to do it well

Come early, wear sun protection, and pace yourself — the Plaza is packed and the desert sun is fierce. Set a budget, but leave room for the piece that stops you in your tracks.

**Field notes**

- Arrive early — the best pieces sell within the first hour.
- Buy directly from artists and ask about their work.
- Sun protection and water are essential in the August heat.

Whether you buy a masterwork or simply witness the spectacle, Indian Market is essential New Mexico — a gathering where a living art world, at its very highest level, spills across a centuries-old plaza.
