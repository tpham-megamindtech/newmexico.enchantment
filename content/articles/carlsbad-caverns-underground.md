The desert above Carlsbad gives no hint of what waits below. Then the ground opens, the trail switchbacks into cool darkness, and within minutes you're descending into a cathedral of stone that took millions of years and a trickle of acidic water to carve.

## Two ways down

You can ride an elevator straight to the Big Room, or you can walk in through the Natural Entrance, dropping 750 feet on a paved switchback as the light fades behind you. Walk in if your knees allow — the transition from sun to gloom is the whole point.

> Underground, the silence has a texture — it presses in gently from every wall.

## The Big Room

At the bottom waits a chamber large enough to swallow several football fields, hung with stalactites and ringed by formations named for their shapes. A mostly level loop trail lets you take it slowly, and slowly is the only sensible speed.

## Stay for the bats

From late spring through October, hundreds of thousands of Brazilian free-tailed bats spiral out of the Natural Entrance at dusk. Rangers give a talk beforehand; phones must be off. It is one of the great wildlife spectacles in the Southwest.

**Field notes**

- The cave holds a steady 56°F year-round; bring a light layer.
- Wear real shoes — the paths are damp and can be slick.
- Reserve timed-entry tickets in advance during summer.

Climbing back into the desert sun feels like surfacing from a dream — one written in limestone and measured in millennia.
