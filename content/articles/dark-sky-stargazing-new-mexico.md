As light pollution swallows the night sky across much of the world, New Mexico has become a refuge for the stars. Its dry air, high elevation, and enormous stretches of near-empty land make it one of the finest places anywhere to look up — and a growing network of dark-sky parks is protecting that view.

## The perfect conditions

Clear, stable, low-humidity air at altitude produces exceptional visibility, while vast rural distances keep the glow of cities at bay. On a moonless night far from town, the Milky Way is bright enough to cast a faint shadow.

> Under a New Mexico sky, the stars aren't a backdrop — they're the main event.

## Certified dark skies

Several New Mexico parks and communities have earned official dark-sky designations, committing to shielded lighting and public astronomy programs. They make ideal, accessible bases for a night under the stars.

## How to look

Give your eyes twenty minutes to adjust, use a red light to preserve your night vision, and go during the new moon for the darkest skies. Even binoculars reveal astonishing detail out here.

**Field notes**

- Plan around the new moon for the darkest skies.
- Let your eyes adjust for 20 minutes; use a red flashlight.
- Seek out certified dark-sky parks for the best views.

In an age of vanishing darkness, New Mexico still offers the real thing: a night sky so full and bright it can stop you in your tracks. Lie back, look up, and remember what humanity has nearly lost.
