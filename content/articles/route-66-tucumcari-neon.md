Route 66 refuses to die in New Mexico, and nowhere is it more gloriously alive than Tucumcari. When the sun drops, the old Mother Road town flickers to life in neon — motels, diners, and curio shops still wearing the optimism of the American mid-century.

## The neon comes on at dusk

Drive Tucumcari Boulevard slowly after dark. The restored signs of a dozen motor courts blaze against the desert sky, and photographers come from around the world to catch them. This is roadside Americana preserved, not staged.

> The Mother Road doesn't just survive here — after dark, it positively glows.

## Murals and memory

By day, the town's murals tell the story of the road that made it: cattle drives, rail lines, and the endless westward hum of tires on asphalt. A slow walk downtown is a tour through a century of travel.

## Where the road goes next

West of Tucumcari the highway runs on toward Santa Rosa and its startling blue swimming hole, then climbs toward Albuquerque. Tucumcari makes the perfect first or last night of a New Mexico road trip.

**Field notes**

- Book a room at one of the restored vintage motels for the full effect.
- Fill the tank; services thin out fast in either direction.
- The Blue Hole in nearby Santa Rosa is an 80-foot-deep artesian spring.

Some towns museum-ify their history. Tucumcari just keeps the lights on — and invites you to pull over and stay the night.
