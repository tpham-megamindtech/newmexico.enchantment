The treatment room used to skew heavily female. Not anymore. Across New Mexico, studios report a steady rise in male clients seeking facials, grooming, and skincare guidance — driven partly by a shifting culture, and partly by a desert climate that spares no one's skin.

## The desert doesn't discriminate

Sun, wind, and dry air are as tough on men's skin as anyone's, and the effects — roughness, sunburn, premature lines — are impossible to ignore. Many men arrive after a hard season outdoors, ready to try something new.

> The high-desert sun has done more to sell men on skincare than any ad campaign ever could.

## Straightforward, not fussy

Studios have learned to meet the moment with no-nonsense approaches: a solid cleanser, sunscreen, a hydrating facial now and then. Framed as maintenance rather than pampering, it's an easy sell.

## Grooming meets skincare

The overlap with the barbershop revival helps; a great fade and a bit of skincare advice increasingly come from the same visit. The stigma, quietly, has all but dissolved.

**Field notes**

- Start simple: cleanser, moisturizer, daily sunscreen.
- A single hydrating facial is an easy, low-commitment intro.
- Barbershops increasingly offer skincare add-ons.

The reluctance is fading fast, and the reason is written on every wind-chapped face. In New Mexico's climate, skincare isn't a luxury or a statement — it's just common sense catching up.
