At first glance, the blinding white dunes of White Sands look utterly barren. Look closer and a remarkable, hidden world emerges — a place where plants race the shifting sand and animals have evolved, in near-record time, to become as white as the ground they live on.

## Evolution you can see

Several species here, from lizards to pocket mice, have evolved pale coloring to blend with the gypsum, a textbook case of natural selection unfolding in an isolated habitat. Their darker cousins live just beyond the dunes.

> The dunes look empty, but they're a living laboratory of evolution in fast-forward.

## Plants that outrun the sand

Some plants survive burial by growing pedestals of hardened sand around their roots as the dunes migrate, standing tall even as the landscape moves beneath them. Others simply grow fast and set seed before they're swallowed.

## A fragile balance

This gypsum dune field is the largest on Earth, and its ecosystem is both unique and delicate. Staying on established routes and leaving everything as you found it keeps the wonder intact for the next visitor.

**Field notes**

- Tread lightly; the dune ecosystem is fragile and unique.
- Dawn and dusk are the best times to spot wildlife.
- It's the largest gypsum dune field on the planet.

White Sands rewards the curious. Beneath its blank, blinding beauty runs a story of adaptation and survival — proof that even the emptiest-looking desert is quietly, stubbornly alive.
