Taos Ski Valley has a reputation, and it earns it. The mountain rises to nearly 12,500 feet and hides some of the most demanding lift-served terrain in the Southern Rockies — but between the legends are wide blue cruisers and a village with real soul.

## Terrain for every nerve

Yes, there's Al's Run and the hike-to ridge chutes that draw expert skiers. But more than half the mountain is intermediate and gentler, and the ski school here has a near-mythic reputation for turning tentative visitors into confident ones.

> Ski it once and Taos stops being a name on a map and becomes a mountain you argue about.

## Thin air, big views

At this altitude the sky goes a deep cobalt and the snow stays cold and dry. Take breaks, hydrate hard, and pause at the top to look out over the Sangre de Cristo range folding away to the horizon.

## Après, New Mexico style

Down in the village the day ends not with generic lodge food but with green-chile stew, a local ale, and the smell of woodsmoke. It's a mountain town that still feels like a discovery.

**Field notes**

- Give yourself a day to acclimate before hitting the steeps.
- Beginner? The learning terrain is genuinely excellent — don't be scared off by the reputation.
- Storms roll in fast; check the avalanche and weather reports.

Long after your legs recover, you'll remember the light on the ridge and that first cold, squeaking turn into untracked snow.
